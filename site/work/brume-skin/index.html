Get found for the problem, not the brand name.

Brume makes a barrier-repair range for sensitive skin. People rarely search for a brand like it by name; they search for the problem, like stinging, tightness or redness, and Brume barely appeared for any of it.

The brief was to grow organic revenue enough to cut paid search on non-brand terms, without publishing anything a dermatologist would wince at.

2024

Three moves.

What we changed, in the order we changed it.

01 / 03

  1. Months 1–2

    Fix what Google could not read

    Filters on the shop were creating 3,200 near-identical pages, and product pages took five seconds to load on a phone. We cleaned the first up and halved the second before writing a word.

    3,200 duplicate pages removed

  2. Months 3–9

    Answer the questions people ask

    Category pages and articles built around real searches, such as why skin stings after cleansing, each one reviewed by a consultant dermatologist and linked to the product that actually helps.

    64 pages written and reviewed

  3. Months 6–14

    Links from people who test skincare

    A study of 1,000 people with sensitive skin gave journalists and reviewers a reason to write about Brume. Each piece linked back to the page that answered the question.

    140 links from 90 publications
